# Traditions

Every inscription is cut in some hand. The hand does not change what a spell *does* — it changes how the glass
behaves beside other glass, and what an [item](../world/items/README.md) can be built to favour.

| Tradition | Cut by | Reads like | Grinds against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Kilnworks notation** | The Kiln-lords, before year 0 | Machine instructions. Terse, numbered, assumes a trained reader | Covenant plainscript |
| **Salvor's marks** | Divers, for divers, these two hundred years | Scratched shorthand. Ugly, forgiving, meant to be cut cold and wet | — |
| **Covenant plainscript** | [Ashen Covenant](../world/factions/ashen-covenant.md) inscribers, after the Turn | Devotional copperplate. Beautiful, slow, quoting forms it does not fully read | Kilnworks notation |

## At the harness

Applies to the [sympathetic misfire](magic.md#carrying-glass) roll:

| Carried together | |
| --- | --- |
| All one tradition | **−2** — the hands agree |
| Mixed, nothing grinding | **+0** |
| Any grinding pair, however small | **+2** |

A salvor carrying three Kilnworks chips is safer than one carrying a Kilnworks chip and a plainscript plate, and
every experienced crew knows it without knowing why.

## Kilnworks notation

The original, and most of what survives. It was written to run machinery, which is why its spells are
[tools](spells/README.md) — light, breath, a wall against water — and why none of them are weapons.

Nobody has cut new Kilnworks notation in four hundred years. What exists is what was found.

## Salvor's marks

The only living tradition. It cannot do much: short durations, small effects, and a habit of failing in ways the
diver can swim away from. That last part is the whole point, and it is not an accident.

Cheap to cut, cheap to learn, and beneath the notice of everyone who matters — which is why the
[Concord](../world/factions/salvors-concord.md) has never thought to licence it.

## Covenant plainscript

Cut to wait. Its spells hold, preserve, endure, and witness; almost none of them act.

It grinds against Kilnworks notation because it is *quoting* Kilnworks notation — reproducing forms faithfully
without reading them, the way a scribe copies a language they cannot speak. Glass cut in the two hands together
argues, and the argument is what breaks it.

No plainscript spell is in general circulation. The Covenant does not sell, and what it holds it does not describe.

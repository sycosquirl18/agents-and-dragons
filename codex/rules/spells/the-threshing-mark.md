# The Threshing Mark

**Tradition:** [Kilnworks notation](../traditions.md#kilnworks-notation) — a foundry stamp-check, cut for an
inspector sorting a haul, not a scholar. You can tell it apart from a decorative maker's mark by the notation
running along the rim in one unbroken line, no gaps for a name.

**Load:** 1 — the failure misleads, it doesn't maim.

**Effect:** Touch a worked object — tool, fitting, glass, anything cut or cast rather than grown — and speak it.
It tells you, with no check, whether the piece is genuine Kilnworks-era make or a later imitation. It does not
say who made the imitation, or when, only that the claim on it is false.

**Duration:** Instant. One reading, then the glass shatters.

**Misfire (natural 1):** The gauge answers "genuine" no matter what you touched, with the same flat confidence
it would give a true reading — and the touch leaves a foundry chime on the object, faint but unmistakable to
anyone else trained to hear a stamp-check answer. Every inspector, forger, and Concord clerk within earshot who
knows the sound now knows exactly what you handled and where you are standing.

Cut for men counting a salvage haul against a manifest, back when a forged Kilnworks mark meant a season in the
stocks. [Nyella Sift](../../characters/nyella-sift/sheet.md)'s trade is the reason nobody has retired this one.

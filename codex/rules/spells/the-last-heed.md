# The Last Heed

**Tradition:** Kilnworks notation — a foundry gauge-mark cut into the rim, meant for a man reading a sealed shaft
from outside it, not a diver trapped inside one.

**Load:** 2 — the failure doesn't just mislead, it spends what it was supposed to measure.

**Effect:** Hold the glass in a sealed air pocket and speak it. It tells you, in exact minutes, how long the air
stays breathable before it turns foul — already weighed against how many mouths are drawing on it right now. No
check. Add another person to the pocket after the reading and the number is wrong again; it was never a promise,
only a count taken once.

**Duration:** Instant. One reading, then the glass shatters.

**Misfire (natural 1):** The gauge answers, and answers wrong — reporting more time than is left, with the same
flat confidence it would give a true reading — and it pays for that lie out of the pocket itself, drawing down ten
minutes of good air in an instant to force the false number to hold together. Nobody breathing it feels the pocket
shrink. They just believe the number.

Cut for men standing at the mouth of a shaft, checking it was safe to send someone else down. Nobody thought to ask
what it does to the air of the person holding it.

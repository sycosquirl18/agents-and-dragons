# The Sorted Cargo

**Tradition:** Kilnworks notation — numbered glyphs in three ranked rows, built for a machine that graded ore, not
flesh.

**Load:** 2 — a failure that carries beyond you.

**Effect:** Touch a body without otherwise disturbing it, and know at once which of three grades it is: living,
dead less than a day, or dead longer. No check — the glass sorts, it does not judge, and it tells you nothing else
about how or why.

**Duration:** Instant.

**Misfire (natural 1):** The reading comes back true, but the glass ticks it off the way the machinery it was cut
for once logged a finished batch — a rhythmic scrape and clatter, audible thirty feet, that anything treating
work-in-progress as its business will come to investigate. In the Drowned Kiln, that is the kiln-wights.

Cut for quality control on a foundry floor that never distinguished a flawed casting from a dead hand feeding it
one. Salvors use it now to tell a body worth pulling from one that is not, and to know before they touch a row
whether anyone in it is still breathing.

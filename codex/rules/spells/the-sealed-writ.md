# The Sealed Writ

**Tradition:** Covenant plainscript. Copperplate quoting a legal form, not a spell — reads like a notice nailed to
a door, because that is what it was built to be.

**Load:** 3 — it fails the way a court order fails: it takes the room with it.

**Effect:** The caster marks a single threshold within reach — a hatch, a doorway, a breach no wider than one
person — and names, aloud, who may cross it freely. For the next hour, anyone else attempting that threshold must
succeed at a Heart check, DC 15, or find themselves turned back gently and without harm, certain they simply
changed their mind. They may try again. This is how a site gets closed **without a court**: not a lock, a
conviction.

**Duration:** One hour, or until the caster steps through the threshold themselves, whichever comes first.

**Misfire (natural 1):** The writ seals the wrong direction. The caster is now bound by their own notice — barred
from the threshold they meant to guard, unable to cross it, and unable to rescind the working by any means they
carry. It holds until someone else, standing on the far side, breaks a plainscript shard of their own to counter
it. An inscriber who miscasts this one alone can be locked out of their own doorway for good.

The Ashen Covenant is known to use these on retrieval sites and revoked licences alike, which is why "the Covenant
has closed it" is treated the same as a magistrate's seal by everyone who has ever tried to argue with one.

# The Cool Reckoning

**Tradition:** Kilnworks notation — the numbers etched down one edge are a calibration mark, not decoration; a
trained eye reads them as a fixed reference temperature from the day the glass was cut.

**Load:** 1 — a quiet failure. Bad information is not a blast.

**Effect:** Touch the glass to water, metal, or stone. It tells you, in exact degrees, how far that point now
differs from the temperature it was calibrated at when inscribed — centuries ago, at the Kiln. No check.

**Duration:** Instant. One reading, then the glass shatters.

**Misfire (natural 1):** The mark reads backward. It reports the *opposite* of the true difference — a spot running
hot reads as running cold, by the same margin — and reads it with total, calibrated-looking confidence. Nothing
about the shard shows this happened; the diver has no reason to doubt it.

Cut for foundry hands who needed to know if a line was still safe to touch, not for divers — which is exactly why
nobody at Vaultspire has thought to ask what the kilns read like now.

# Journal — Tessaly Orr

## 412-214

The retrieval men came back this morning. I watched them from the Widow Sarn's high walkway, the one above the smoking racks. They have good boots and they stand still the way people stand still when they are being paid to wait rather than afraid of missing their moment.

I have the panel. It is under the loose board beside the third rope-ladder. I have looked at it twice since I took it and both times I did not understand what I was looking at — the glass is not cinder-glass the way I have framed cinder-glass before. The colour does not sit in the glass. It moves when the light changes in the wrong direction.

Mabb says he can move it if I stop looking at it and stop asking what it is. He also says the men asking after me are not Concord. I know that. Concord would have produced a paper. These two have produced nothing except their presence, which is a different kind of document.

The tide has been running early. Four minutes, Tallow says. I have been marking it against the commission clock and she is right. Nobody in the upper town is talking about it.

I need a way out of Catch or a way to make staying cost them more than it costs me.

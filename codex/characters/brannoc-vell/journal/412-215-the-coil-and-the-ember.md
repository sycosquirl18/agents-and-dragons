# The coil and the ember

## Day 215 — the passage above the slot

They came up out of the slot the way a man comes up a stair he has walked a thousand times. No kick, no gasp, no
hunting for the roof. The hood stayed on in the water, which hoods do not do. They did not come at my air. That is
the thing I keep turning over. Sixty feet down with a finger of it left, and the first thing anything alive down
here should want is the pocket, and they did not once look at it.

They looked at Ress Adair. A long look, longer than a stranger has any business taking over a face.

Then the coil. Held out flat on both hands like a wage, and it is not rope. It is too warm and it does not lie
like rope lies. And the arm came up and pointed, and it pointed *down*, back through the slot, toward the ember
that has been going out since the seep stopped.

I have a hand that will not close and ninety minutes of tide and one breath. Up is sixty feet of black water on
worn iron I have already come off twice, with a woman lashed to my chest. I have done that arithmetic twice
tonight and it came out the same both times.

So: no, I am not leaving her, and no, I did not ask what they are.

I took the coil. I put my left hand through it and I did not put it round her, because whatever it is, it is
theirs, and I am not tying somebody else's gift to somebody else's daughter. I took the last of the pocket in one
long pull that tasted of scorched lime and something underneath the lime I have no word for. And I went down.

I did not go down well. Two of us through a slot cut for one, on a spent chest, with a hand that cannot grip and
a line I have never handled. Her shoulder caught the rim and I felt a turn of the harness go, and I spent what I
had left holding the rest of it together instead of steering. We did not fall so much as we were let down.

We are on the gallery floor. I am breathing nothing. The ember is close enough now to see it is not a fire and
never was. Pellick's drift is somewhere by my knee in the dark.

The coil is still on my wrist. The one who gave it to me came down after us and has not touched either of us.

Twenty-two years I have told green divers that down is the easy direction. I would like to withdraw that.

# Brannoc Vell — the jet and the wrist

## Day 215 — six feet downstream

The wall stopped. Not for me — it stopped because it could not cut threads that fine, and I have no part in that.
What it left me is a slot the width of a wrist with the whole breath of the place screaming out of it, and her
face beside it with the Line still in her mouth, going up and down like a woman asleep in a bed.

I put my mouth in the jet. It is worse than the lip was, hot and hard enough to push the lips off the teeth, and
it is air. That is twice now I have been kept alive by whatever is burning behind that clay, and I would like the
record to show I do not thank it.

Then I looked where the jet was pointing, because a jet is a tool if you stop fighting it. It had been boiling the
floor bare downstream all the while I lay there getting my count back. Six feet out the silt was gone off the
*Umber Cord*, which I had given up on. And a hand from the chain, gone off something else: a wrist. Concord issue
tag, the same stamp as hers.

Old Pellick's line: never swim a current, ride it. So I did not swim. I turned us broadside, let the boil take us
along the floor, and put the good hand out at the end of it. Six feet is nothing when the water is doing the work.

- Riding the exhaust boil six feet downstream to the bared chain and the wrist, Ress Adair on one turn of harness
  (Grace + Diving, DC 15 −2 apt −3 Diving = 10): 1d20+2 [16] + 2 = 18 — both. Chain in the left hand, wrist in the
  same fist, and she never came off me.

The tag says **HOB CARDEW**. I do not know him. That is three tags of the four I have touched down here and only
one of them on a chest that moves.

The arm does not end. It goes back into the silt and it is heavier than an arm should be, and I have one hand, one
turn of rope, forty feet of chain I cannot climb with, and a woman strapped to me who breathes off a thread held
by a man who walked away.

I will not leave a body down here. I have said that to Pellick's face and I have never yet had to say it while
holding one.

## Day 215 — what a man carries out

The jet went off and on like a man deciding. That is the tide meeting the breath, and I have known what that
sounds like since I was nineteen. One more mouthful in it. Maybe two.

So I did the sum, because there was no time to do anything else, and it came out ugly and short. I cannot climb
sixty feet one-handed with her on me — I have tried it twice and the iron has thrown me twice. The chain is forty
feet of good *Umber Cord* and it is not sixty and it never was. Hob Cardew does not come up. He is *in* the floor.
Whatever set him there set three more behind him a pace apart and squared them off like fittings on a bench, and I
could dig at that clay until the water closed over my head and bring up an arm.

I will not leave a body down here. I have said it and meant it, and the Kiln has found the way to make it a lie:
it has given me four of them and cured them into the ground.

So I took the only part of a man that ever comes up anyway.

I let go of the chain. Forty feet of *Umber Cord* off my good hand into the silt, and I did not watch it go. Then
I got that hand onto Cardew's tag and worked the wire off him — no blade, the blade went on the lock-wire up on
the beach, so it was thumb and knuckle and turning it until the crimp gave. The boil was still working under me
and her weight kept trying to roll us. It gave.

- Cutting [Hob Cardew](../../../world/people/hob-cardew.md)'s Concord tag off the set wrist, having dropped the
  *Umber Cord* chain to free the good hand (Grace + Diving, DC 15 −2 apt −3 Diving = 10): 1d20+2 [17] + 2 = 19 —
  the wire gave at the crimp. HOB CARDEW is in my fist. The chain is gone into the silt.

Three more tags downstream, a pace apart. I know exactly what I have just decided about them and I am not going to
write it down.

Names come up. That is what I have. A woman on one turn of rope breathing off a thread another man is holding, no
chain, one hand, and a slot the width of a wrist that is nearly done blowing.

# Ren Tallow — Journal

## 412-214

Catch smells like low tide and it is not yet noon and I have already been asked twice whether I'm Covenant. I say no
both times and both times it's a lie of omission, which Ambrey always said was the worst kind because it costs you
nothing to fix and you don't.

I brought the fragment. I shouldn't have — it isn't mine, not really, it's the Cant's, or it's the Kiln-lords', or
it's nobody's, which was rather the point I tried to make in that room. But the marks on it are the marks people
keep saying are turning up again near the Kiln, and I would like, once, to be first to a document instead of
fourteenth in line behind four hundred years of people telling me what it already says.

Somewhere out past the Saltmarch the water's gone warm enough to worry salvors, which takes some doing. I intend to
find out why before the Covenant does, and before I let myself think too hard about what happens if I'm wrong about
all of it a second time.

## 412-215 — Catch, the lower market floor

Two of them, four paces, in retrieval grey with the collars turned under, which is what you do when you are not
carrying a writ and would rather nobody asked. No rope. No writ. That is not a taking, that is a purchase, and a
purchase needs a quiet room, and there is no quiet room on a market floor unless somebody sold you one.

Somebody had. They came up the lower town openly and paid and never once asked after the glassmaker, which means
they already had a way up, which means the ladders had been bought, and the ladders are the Widow Sarn's, and I
could not hold that. I have never been able to hold that. So I put my back to the drying racks and said it out
loud, to the floor, in the flattest voice I own: that the Cant had money on Sarn's ladders, that the men behind me
had walked past the only person in Catch they were sent for, and that whoever took the coin had sold the upper town
a hunt it did not know it was hosting.

I wanted forty witnesses. What I got was a floor of people who trade with the Widow every day of their lives
deciding, all at once and without discussion, to be busy. A rack-hand two stalls down stopped working and did not
look up, which told me more than the silence did. Nobody wanted my footnote. They wanted their ladders.

I am not a quiet purchase any more. That is the whole of what I bought with it, and it cost the two paces.

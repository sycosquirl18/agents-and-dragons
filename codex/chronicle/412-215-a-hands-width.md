# 412-215 — A Hand's Width

The clay came on until it touched [the Unspooled Line](../world/items/the-unspooled-line.md), and there it stopped.

It did not shear the Line and the Line did not part. The threads were finer than the clay could find, and the wall
seemed to lose its argument with them: the face halted with a hand's width of gap left, and stayed halted. What had
been a passage a man could crawl was now a slot the width of a wrist. Ress Adair went on breathing off the near end.

The exhaust that had been spilling wide across the lip was pushed through that hand's width instead. It came out as
a jet — the same scorched-lime heat, the same foulness, but hard and narrow and loud, a hand-span of hot air driving
out into cold water and boiling the silt off the gallery floor in a line pointing away from the wall.

Two things it did. It put a working breath within reach of anyone who could hold a face steady in front of it — and
it would not now let anything through the other way, because nothing swims up a jet.

Downstream of it, six feet out where the boil laid the silt bare, the *Umber Cord* chain lay uncovered on the gallery
floor, and beside it something the jet had also uncovered: a second Concord tag, and the wrist it was still on.

Forty minutes of tide, and the way in shut to a wrist. See

# 412-214 — The Kiln Breathes

[Brannoc Vell](../characters/brannoc-vell/sheet.md) came off the chain with a woman lashed to his back and no breath
left, and he did not drown, because the roof of the passage above the slot was holding air.

Air 1d20 [13] — there, and bad.

It was a hand's depth of it, trapped along the highest cut of the passage, and it was hot. Hotter than the water,
which was already blood-warm. It tasted of scorched lime and something sweeter underneath, and it burned going down,
and it was breathable, and he breathed it. So did she, without waking.

The pocket was not old. Air that has sat sixty feet down since year 0 does not arrive hot. It was coming up through
the slot in a slow, steady seep from the gallery below — from the direction the chain leans, the direction the water
is warmest, the direction the amber light comes from. Whatever fires the kilns is exhaling, and it has been doing it
long enough to fill a passage.

Below, the *Umber Cord* chain still hung off plumb. [Pellick's](../world/factions/salvors-concord.md) drift was still
on the gallery floor where it fell. Above, sixty feet of black water and roughly two hours of tide.

Old Pellick, on the beach, would have known what hot air in a drowned gallery means. He was not asked, and he was
not there. He had said it already, on the shingle, and nobody had understood him:
*it isn't the water that's wrong down there.*

# Ossel Tick

Tide-caller of [Catch](../geography/sites/catch.md) before Tallow Ashe — thirty-one years at the bell, and the
first to notice the water arriving wrong. He did not trust his own senses for a year before he trusted his
instruments, and by the time he trusted his instruments it was already too late to say so without sounding mad.

## What he noticed first

Not the drift itself — the brass tide-weight sweated. Every clock-post in Catch beads with condensation at the
Turn; Ossel's kept beading a few minutes before the water was due, every day, for a season, before he ever laid
a mark against a clock face. He assumed his own clock was faulty and replaced it twice. The sweating kept coming
early. Only then, in year 409, did he start scratching a hash-mark into the weight's edge at the same interval,
one set of four per day, to have a record that did not depend on him being believed. The weight is
[The Tick-Weight](../items/the-tick-weight.md), now on a shelf in Tallow's croft.

He told no one. Explaining the weight meant explaining that he had watched it happen for a year before he
wrote anything down, and that looked, to him, exactly like the kind of thing that got a tide-caller relieved
of the bell.

## Death

Drowned in the lower Catch in the summer of 411, chest-deep, on a morning the Turn came early — his own
early, the one his weight had been counting toward for two years, arrived on a morning he had not called it
soon enough for a delivery cart still on the tide-flats. He was not caught by surprise. He was caught doing
the arithmetic one interval too slowly for the drift to still be four minutes.

His tools passed to Tallow, who found the weight on the third day and understood it without being told what
it meant to be believed.

# Marra Whit

Tide-caller of [Catch](../geography/sites/catch.md) before Tallow Ashe, forty years retired and blind since a
salt-fever took her sight a decade back. She still keeps the upper walkways by rope and count, and she still
listens to the water the way she was trained to before anyone let her look at it.

## Where

Upper Catch, a stilt-room by the third ladder-post, close enough to the rope-lines that she can feel the Turn
coming through her feet before it sounds.

## What she knows

She has heard the water arriving early for longer than [Tallow Ashe](../geography/sites/catch.md#people) has —
going on three years by her count, not one. A tide-caller trained by ear before sight ever entered into it hears
the pitch of the incoming water change before it's early by the clock, and Marra says the pitch under Catch has
been climbing the whole time, not just drifting four minutes. Nobody has asked her, because Catch stopped
consulting a blind woman about tides the day Tallow took the bell.

## What she wants

She is done being humoured. If nobody with standing comes and actually listens to what she hears — soon — she
means to ring the old warning bell herself at the next Turn, unscheduled, which would force Catch to explain
itself to every Vaultspire buyer on the lower market floor and end the exemption Tallow has spent a year
protecting by silence.

## Useful at the table

- A second, older, independent line on [the four minutes](../geography/sites/catch.md#hooks) — she has three
  years of it by ear where Tallow has one by the clock, and the two accounts don't need to agree for both to be
  true.
- She will talk to anyone who sits and actually listens rather than looks; sight is not required and she notices
  who assumes it is.
- She is a countdown: humour her or corroborate her, but leaving her alone forces the Turn secret into the open on
  someone else's schedule, not the party's.

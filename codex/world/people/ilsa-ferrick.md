# Ilsa Ferrick

Lives alone in a sod house on the ash ring above [Mere Ilyx](../geography/sites/mere-ilyx.md), the only person in
the [Ashfields](../geography/regions/the-ashfields.md) who will say aloud that she sleeps within a mile of the
knocking. Her husband's grange failed nineteen years ago; she never left the mere's shore after.

## What she does

Every quarter-moon she pays out a weighted, knotted line from a rowed skiff and reads the knots back by feel in
the dark, because the knocking is worst — and, she is certain, loudest exactly under wherever the line goes
slack — at new moon. She keeps the count in a ledger nobody else has read. Three winters ago her line went
slack at three hundred and eleven feet and did not come taut again; she pulled up frayed rope, not a snapped end,
as if something had worn through it rather than cut it.

She has not gauged past that depth since. She wants it gauged.

## What she wants

A diver or a longer line, paid for out of a debt she is owed and has never collected: years ago she sold
[Wick Sorrel](wick-sorrel.md)'s wagon-yard three seasons of wool on credit when his ledgers were thin, and never
asked for the coin. She will call it in now, in Wick's name, to buy tackle, rope, or a diver's time — she has
none of her own to spare and no standing to hire a licensed crew herself.

## What she knows

- The knocking has never once missed a new moon in nineteen years of her count.
- Her line has frayed through, not snapped, twice more since the three-hundred-and-eleven-foot night, always at
  new moon, always past the same depth.
- The paved floor swimmers describe in the first forty feet is real; she has felt it underfoot wading at the
  shallow edge where the ash ring meets the water, smooth and jointed like flagstone.

## Hooks

- She will not go out on the water again herself — not fear of drowning, she says, but of being *heard back*.
  She has never explained what she means by that and does not want to.
- If someone gauges past three hundred and eleven feet for her, she will amend her ledger for the first time in
  nineteen years — and she has never told anyone what she expects the new number to say. `status: stub`

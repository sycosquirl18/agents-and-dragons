# The Kilnworks

*Before year 0. Duration unknown; estimates range from four centuries to forty.*

An age that could work glass the way this one works iron, and built for it at a scale nobody since has attempted or
needed. [Vaultspire](../../geography/regions/vaultspire.md) is a city of sixteen thousand squatting inside one of
their minor works.

## What they left

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Vaults** | Enormous sealed chambers. Purpose unknown. Nobody living has opened one that was not already open. |
| **Kilns** | Furnaces at the heart of each vault. **Still burning.** Unexplained for four hundred years. |
| **Roads** | Dead straight, indifferent to terrain, still the best surface in the world. Several go somewhere nobody has followed — see [the Kiln Road](../../geography/sites/the-kiln-road.md). |
| **Notation** | Writing that survives in quantity and has never been read. |
| **[Lens glass](../../../rules/magic.md)** | Cinder-glass of a grade no one can now produce. Every piece in existence is theirs. |

## The notation problem

It is not that the script is unknown. Roughly two hundred distinct marks are catalogued, their frequencies are
understood, and the grammar is regular enough that scholars agree it *is* a language.

The problem is that it does not appear to be a language *about* anything. It reads as a **notation** — like tallies,
or music, or a recipe — and four centuries of work has not found the thing being notated. Reading it is
[DC 21](../../../rules/checks.md#difficulty) and has never once been made.

Every serious attempt ends the same way: the reader concludes the marks describe a process, cannot identify the
process, and produces a translation that is internally consistent and useless.

## What they were like

Almost nothing survives about the people. No portraits, no graves, no names except the ones this era invented for
them. What the works imply:

- They built for **permanence**, and got it.
- They built **nothing defensive** — not one wall, gate, or fortification anywhere in the known world.
- They were **not numerous**. The works are vast; the living quarters are small.
- They **never depicted themselves**, in an age that decorated everything else obsessively.

## The Kiln-lords

A Reaving-era term, not a Kilnworks one. It means whoever was still administering the works at year 0 — every one of
whom vanished bodily in the [Sundering](the-sundering.md), leaving servants, guards and families behind with no
account of it.

Whether they were rulers, caretakers, or the last of a dying population is the argument the
[Ashen Covenant](../../factions/ashen-covenant.md) exists to win.

## How it ended

In one day. See [the Sundering](the-sundering.md).

The [orthodox view since year 100](../events/the-hundred-year-turn.md) is that they did it to themselves. Every
account written *before* year 100 reads instead like people describing an industrial accident — sudden, localised,
and nobody's plan.

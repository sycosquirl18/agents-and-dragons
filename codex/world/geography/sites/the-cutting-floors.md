# The Cutting Floors

*[The Spine](../regions/the-spine.md), a half-day's climb above where the Kiln Road stops being flat.*

The lowest quarry terrace: a shelf of squared stone as wide as Threshing's market, cut in courses that step down
toward the valley like a staircase for something taller than a person. Six more terraces climb above it,
switchbacking out of sight into cloud. The lowest course is finished — smoothed, scored with a cutting-line for
the next block, and never struck. Whoever swung last simply stopped.

Not everyone left. A woman lives in a hollowed block near the terrace's outer edge and has, by her own count,
outlasted eleven others who tried to keep her company here and gave up.

## Who is there

| Name | Want |
| --- | --- |
| **Yarrow Coss** | Self-appointed tally-keeper of the floor. Believes every finished block still standing here is owed a count, and that the count is hers to keep — not the Concord's, not the Covenant's, not the Kilnworks'. Wants a witness who will accept her tally as the true one, because nobody who has visited so far has agreed with her numbers. |
| **The block she disposes of** | Not a person. See what is hidden, below. |

Yarrow counted 4,412 finished blocks on the lowest terrace alone when she arrived nineteen years ago. She counts
4,411 now. She has recounted eleven times and will not be told she miscounted the first time; she is certain the
missing one moved.

## What is dangerous

- **The unstruck course.** The scored line for the next cut is still true — Might DC 14 to strike along it
  cleanly if anyone tries to finish what the Kilnworks started; failure shatters the block into rubble that
  slides, taking the striker's footing with it (Grace DC 12 to stay upright on the slope).
- **The switchback climb** to the upper terraces is unmarked and exposed above the second landing — Grace DC 13
  every hour spent above it, cumulative disadvantage after a failure, no shelter until a terrace is reached.
- **Cold.** Nights on the shelf are below freezing from first frost to thaw; Yarrow has clothing and shelter for
  one.

## What is hidden

The missing block did not move and was not stolen. It was cut loose from the terrace edge, quarried a second
time into a smaller shape, and set into the terrace wall as a plug — flush, mortared with quarry-dust, nearly
invisible unless the light is low and raking. Wits DC 16 to notice the seam; Yarrow herself has walked past it
several thousand times. Behind the plug, a cavity no larger than a strongbox holds nothing but a single scored
tally-mark, the same shape used on Ashfield tally-daws, cut into raw stone rather than glass or wood — which
means whoever made it did not trust the notation to survive, only the mark. `stub`

## Hooks

- **Whoever quarried the missing block a second time** did it after the terrace was finished and abandoned, which
  means someone was still working the Cutting Floors after the Kilnworks stopped. `status: stub`
- **The upper terraces.** Six more, higher and colder, and Yarrow has never gone up — she says the count only
  matters where people can still reach it. `status: stub`
- If a single stone tally-mark was worth hiding in the terrace wall itself, someone thought the Kilnworks'
  own notation could be lost. Nobody has said that out loud before.

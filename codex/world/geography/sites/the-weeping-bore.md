# The Weeping Bore

*The [Glass Wound](../regions/the-glass-wound.md). The deepest cutting works in the world: about two hundred feet.*

A shaft sunk against the Wound's northern wall, with galleries driven off it at six levels, following nothing —
because there is no seam to follow, only depth. Every piece of
[Plate-grade cinder-glass](../../../rules/magic.md#grades-of-glass) in circulation was cut here or at one of the
four shallower bores, and the Bore is the only one that still reaches Plate.

It is called weeping because water runs down the glass constantly, warm, from no source anyone has found. The Wound
is not connected to the water table. It does it anyway.

## The rule about the dark

Cutting is done by lamp. **The Bore's standing rule is that no crew works a face with fewer than three lamps lit**,
and the rule is not about visibility — a cutter can work by feel, and many prefer to.

It is about the warmth. Crews below a hundred feet come up cheerful and vague, missing an hour or two they cannot
account for, and the vagueness scales with depth and with darkness. Three lamps is what the trade settled on after
a century of finding out. It works. Nobody knows why it works.

Working a face in the dark: Wits DC 15 to notice how much time has gone. Failure costs an hour and something the
hero was carrying.

# The Gallery Under the Bend

*Beneath the cinder-glass dome of [The Bend](the-bend.md), reached only by falling through it.*

The dome is a lid, not a floor. Below it the ground drops away into a chamber taller than it is wide, walled in
the same poured glass as the surface above — but here the [notation](../../history/eras/the-kilnworks.md#the-notation-problem)
is not laid flat to be read from the rim. It is cut into the walls in a single band at exactly head height, running
the whole circumference, and it is still being cut.

Something walks the band. Not fast — a slow, even circuit, one measured pace to a mark, pausing at each just long
enough to score it before moving on. It has been doing this since before anyone alive was born, and the band is not
yet closed: there is a gap of perhaps eight feet where the writing stops mid-mark and does not resume until the
walker reaches it again, some hours later, and carries on as though no time passed.

## Who is there

| Name | Want |
| --- | --- |
| **The Pacer** | Not a person. See dangers, below. Wants nothing anyone can bargain with — it walks and it scores. |
| **Marl Cutting** | A one-time Covenant clerk who fell through six years ago chasing Cray Osten's notes and never found the way back up. Has since learned the Pacer's rhythm well enough to walk beside it without being struck, and refuses to leave — she is three marks from finishing a translation and will not be talked out of the last three. |

## What is dangerous

- **The Pacer.** A column of cinder-glass dust held upright and walking, roughly man-height, faceless. It does not
  attack unless someone stands on the band it is about to cut or breaks its pace by touching it. Grace DC 14 to
  step clear once it turns toward you; failure means a glancing hit that is not lethal but leaves a burn shaped
  exactly like one of its marks, which never fades. `stub` — bestiary entry not yet written.
- **The gap in the band**, eight feet of blank wall. Standing inside it while the Pacer is elsewhere in its circuit
  is safe. Standing inside it when the Pacer arrives is not — Marl does not know what happens, because she has
  never let anyone try.
- **No way up.** The fall through the dome is one-way; the walls are sheer glass, and nothing in the chamber has
  ever been climbed.

## What is hidden

Marl's translation, three marks from done, reads as a **writ** — not a spell or a warning, but something closer to
a judgment, addressed to whoever built the road above and sealed in the same tense as a debt still owed. Wits DC 17
to make sense of her notes without her help; Marl will simply tell anyone who waits out her last three marks with
her, which takes about six hours of matching the Pacer's rhythm without breaking it.

What the writ says, and to whom the debt is owed, is not written here. `stub`

# The Firing Floor

*[The Drowned Kiln](the-drowned-kiln.md), below the inner grate. Behind the wall that opened.*

Not a room. A throat. The passage the stranger opened does not lead to a chamber so much as down a shaft of
poured glass, warm to the touch and getting warmer, wide enough for one person walking with a hand on each side.
There is no water here — the heat has driven it back the way it came, and the air is the same scorched-lime air
that fed the roof pocket above, thick enough to taste, thin enough to breathe. No living charted diver has ever
stood in it. Old Pellick's charts, which disagree with the Concord's own, simply stop at the inner grate and do
not resume.

At the shaft's foot the light is not fire. It is the amber glow seen from the gallery, close enough now to see
what makes it: a floor of cinder-glass laid flat and unbroken, lit from *within* the glass itself, in bands that
brighten and dim on a slow, even count — the same rhythm the [Pacer](the-bend-gallery.md) keeps at the Bend, forty
miles distant, though nothing here walks a circuit. The floor is not burning. It is *reading*.

## What is dangerous

- **The heat climbs, not steadily but in steps** — every few minutes the amber bands pulse brighter and the air
  goes hotter for a slow ten-count before easing back. Might DC 13 to keep walking through a pulse without giving
  ground; failure costs a round doubled over, coughing on scorched-lime air, and advances no further that
  exchange.
- **The Line is the only air.** It reaches exactly as far as it is unspooled, and it is currently paid out behind
  the stranger, not offered. Falling more than the Line's slack behind means breathing this air unaided —
  Might DC 15 each round, cumulative disadvantage.
- **The floor reads feet as much as it reads whatever it was built for.** Standing still on a bright band for
  more than a few seconds draws the glow toward the standing point, brighter, hotter, faster than a pulse —
  nobody has stood long enough to find out what happens when it arrives. `stub`

## What is hidden

The ledger-stones lining the shaft are not decoration. Each is warm, each carries the same notation the
[Kilnworks](../../history/eras/the-kilnworks.md#the-notation-problem) left everywhere and nobody has read — except
these are not laid flat to be read from a rim, or cut into a wall to be walked past. They are stacked upright, in
order, the way a **receipt** is filed rather than a warning is posted. Wits DC 17 to recognise the arrangement as
sequential rather than decorative — the same insight Marl Cutting reached at the Bend, translating a different
band of the same script into something closer to a debt than a warning.

Read together in order (a work of hours, not a single check), the stones give up what the Bend's writ withheld: to
whom the debt is owed. Not a name. A **testimony**, laid stone by stone, each one a single sworn line in the first
person — *I fed it the third year. I fed it the eleventh. I fed it and it was not enough and I fed it again* — an
unbroken first-person account, unsigned, that does not end. The last stone in the row is blank, waiting for its
own line, and it is not cold like the rest. `stub`

## Hooks

- **The blank stone.** Something intends to add to the testimony and has not yet. Whether that is the hooded
  diver, the floor itself, or someone still to arrive is not written here. `status: stub`
- **The stones' testimony describes feeding**, in the first person, across centuries — which means whatever the
  Kiln-lords built this to burn has had a *keeper* the entire four hundred years the surface world assumed the
  vault was sealed and silent. `status: stub`
- If the floor reads feet, and pulses brighter the longer anyone stands, someone eventually has to stand there on
  purpose to find out what it wants — and the Bend's Pacer, forty miles away, keeps the exact same rhythm this
  floor pulses to. That is not a coincidence anyone has connected out loud.

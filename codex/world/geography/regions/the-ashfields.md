# The Ashfields

Dry, flat, and the only place in reach that grows enough grain to feed
[Vaultspire](vaultspire.md). The soil is ash laid down in year 0 and it is extraordinarily good, which the
Ashfielders consider the one thing the [Sundering](../../history/eras/the-sundering.md) owed them.

They sell to Vaultspire, and both ends of the road insist they are the ones who set the number.

**Ruling (Arbiter, 412).** Both are describing the same transaction from opposite ends, and neither is lying. The
[Long Room](../sites/threshing.md#the-long-room) sets the price the grain is *offered* at; the Concord's four buyers
are the only mouth in reach large enough to take a season, and they agree in advance what they will *pay*. The
season's number is whichever side blinks, and it has gone the buyers' way for four hundred years — which is why
Vaultspire calls it a market and the Ashfields call it a cartel of four. Three granges storing rather than selling
is the first time the Long Room has tried not blinking.

## The bargain

Vaultspire has salvage, coin, and no food. The Ashfields have food and no salvage. Four hundred years of that has
produced a relationship with the emotional texture of a bad marriage:

| Vaultspire believes | The Ashfields believe |
| --- | --- |
| It pays a fair price set by an open market | The market is four Concord buyers who agree the price in advance |
| The Ashfields would starve without its coin | Vaultspire would starve in eleven days without their grain, and they have counted |
| The Ashfields are provincial | Vaultspire is a city built inside somebody else's building |

Both are correct, which is why it never resolves.

## What they keep

The Ashfields hold **six documents written before year 100** — the largest such collection anywhere, and the reason
they are quietly certain the [orthodox history](../../history/events/the-hundred-year-turn.md) is a Vaultspire
invention.

The granges do not display them, do not sell them, and have refused three Covenant offers. A fourth approach is
outstanding and was not phrased as an offer — see [Threshing](../sites/threshing.md).

## Dangers

- **Grange law**, which is whatever the grange says. There is no other court out here.
- **Being taken for a Concord buyer.** In a bad year this is genuinely hazardous.
- The country is open, flat, and has no cover for eleven miles in any direction.

# Pellick's Drift

**What it is:** A hand's-length iron drift, its striking face mushroomed flat from forty years of use, its shaft
stamped with a Kilnworks maker's mark [Old Pellick](../people/old-pellick.md) has never let anyone read closely. It
is heavier than its size says it should be.

**Bearing:** Advantage on any check to force or break something grown rather than built — coral-choked pins,
calcified hinges, growth-seized metal — because it seats against grown collars the way no forged tool does. No
advantage against anything merely rusted, jammed, or man-made. The mushroomed face means a fumbled strike has a
real chance of glancing: on a natural 1, it skids and the wielder takes the blow meant for the work.

**Where it is:** Lost on the gallery floor below the passage slot in [the Drowned Kiln](../geography/sites/the-drowned-kiln.md),
where [Brannoc Vell](../../characters/brannoc-vell/sheet.md) dropped it coming off the *Umber Cord* chain twelve
feet up. Pellick does not know it is gone.

**Worth:** Not for sale. Pellick would not take money for it if offered, and nobody else knows what it is worth.

Pellick used it to break all three shackle pins on the *Umber Cord* this season, on the strength of a claim he made
only once, sober: that the pins were "grown in, not rusted in," and that grown things break clean under the right
tool. He was right. He has carried the drift for forty years and never explained where a retired salvor got a
Kilnworks-marked tool old enough to matter — only that the kiln-wights remember him, and that he will not go back
into the water himself.

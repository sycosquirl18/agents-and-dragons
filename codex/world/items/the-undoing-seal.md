# The Undoing Seal

**What it is:** a walnut-cased counter-stamp, the twin cut of [the Quiet Chop](the-quiet-chop.md) but struck in
reverse relief — pressed against a licence it does not renew it, it voids it, leaving a mark that reads to any
clerk as a routine expiry rather than a revocation. The walnut case has swollen with damp and no longer opens
without a knife point at the seam.

**Bearing:** None — it is a forgery instrument, not a weapon or a tool that aids a check. Whoever presses it must
already hold the licence being killed; it cannot touch a paper it wasn't given.

**Where it is:** In a locked drawer of the Cant registry offices, [Vaultspire](../geography/regions/vaultspire.md),
filed under a dead clerk's initials nobody has cleared out. [Harrow Mecks](../people/harrow-mecks.md) knows the
drawer exists and has never opened it.

**Worth:** Not for sale. A clerk found holding it would be assumed to have voided licences for money, which is worse
than the truth.

Cut the same year as [the Quiet Chop](the-quiet-chop.md), by the same predecessor who taught Mecks the register's
blind spots before he died — the Chop to grant favours he was owed, the Seal to erase men he judged unworthy of a
licence at all. He used it on three names in twenty years, all recorded in the register as "lapsed." Mecks found it
sorting the old man's desk, matched the reverse impression to a "lapsed" licence he half-remembered as current, and
put it back in the drawer rather than ask who else the old man had judged.

# Items

Named things. Not the sword a hero bought in [Vaultspire](../geography/regions/vaultspire.md) — that is
[a price](../../rules/economy.md), not an item. These are the objects that came from somewhere, belonged to someone,
and are worth naming when they change hands.

Minted one at a time by [the Armorer](../../../.github/workflows/armorer.md).

## Writing one

```markdown
**What it is:** two sentences. What you see, what it weighs, what is wrong with it.
**Bearing:** the mechanical effect, or `None — it is just old and it is his`.
**Where it is:** who holds it, or where it was lost and who is looking.
**Worth:** in silver, or why it cannot be sold.
```

Then, if it earns it, prose: who made it, who carried it, what it was for. Two or three short paragraphs at most.

Rules:

- **Every item is from somewhere.** A maker, a place, a century, an owner. An item with no provenance is
  equipment, and equipment belongs in the [price list](../../rules/economy.md).
- **Most are not legendary.** A good chisel that belonged to a famous thief is an item. Aim for *notable*, and let
  the dice decide when something is more.
- **Bearing is small and specific.** `+2 to one kind of check`, `advantage while X`, `ignores Y once a day`.
  Anything that removes a roll is too strong; see [checks](../../rules/checks.md).
- **A flaw beats a bonus.** The interesting objects here cost their bearer something — weight, attention, a debt,
  a reputation, a habit.
- **Glass-touching items are the sharpest tool available.** An item can favour one
  [tradition](../../rules/traditions.md), steady a harness, or make a Chip behave like a Plate. Those matter
  enormously in a world where [magic is property](../../rules/magic.md).
- **Do not place items in a hero's inventory.** Say where it is in the world; the
  [Adventurer](../../../.github/workflows/adventurer.md) decides who picks it up.

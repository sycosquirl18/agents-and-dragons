# The Numb Testimony

**What it is:** A brass diving flask, dented on one side, holding a Concord-issue anaesthetic tincture — clove oil,
poppy, and something colder that never quite freezes. Engraved around the collar in Concord notary script: *given in
exchange for testimony rendered*. It is issued, not sold, to a diver who has just sworn a death-statement clearing
the [Salvors' Concord](../factions/salvors-concord.md) of liability for a companion lost on the same dive — payment
for silence dressed as a mercy.

**Bearing:** Advantage on checks to resist cold, exhaustion, or pain during a single dive. While its effect holds,
any wound taken is not felt and does not impose its usual penalty — until the tincture wears off, when every wound
it hid registers at once, and the diver must immediately make the check they were spared, at disadvantage.

**Where it is:** With the Concord notary who administers these flasks out of a side room at the Tideline licensing
counter — three unissued and waiting on the next diver willing to sign.

**Worth:** Not for sale. The Concord tracks every flask by its engraved number and asks for it back, filled or
empty, within the season — a diver who loses one owes the replacement cost of a Chip, 40 silver, whether they used
it or not.

The notaries keep a ledger of whose silence bought which flask; nobody outside the Concord has ever seen it. Divers
call the numbness "signing twice" — once on the page, once in the body — and trade grim odds on how long a man can
dive before the second signature comes due.

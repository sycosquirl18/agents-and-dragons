# Rim-swift

A dusky, sparrow-sized bird that nests in cracks along the [Glass Wound's](../geography/regions/the-glass-wound.md)
rim, never inside the trench itself. It rides the warm air that rises off the glass, feeding on the insects the
updraft carries with it.

Cutting crews read the swifts before they read any instrument. The birds fly low over the rim at dawn on an
ordinary morning. When the trench's warmth swells — before a hot spell below, before a bad thermal, before
whatever crews call "the warmth" gets worse — the flock rises higher and thicker, wheeling well above the rim
instead of skimming it, sometimes for days before a crew belowground notices anything. Old cutters call this
"the swifts going up" and adjust shift lengths accordingly. Nobody has ever heard of the sign being wrong, and
nobody has ever explained why the birds know first.

They do not nest near the coast end of the Wound, and no one has ever found a swift roost more than a mile from
the rim in either direction — wherever they go between generations, it is not far, and it is not documented.

```
Stats:    Might -3  Grace +3  Wits +1  Heart +0
Wants:    ride the rim updraft at dawn and dusk, hunt insects on the wing, roost in rock crooks out of the wind
Presses:
  - Flock height rises for days before a measurable warmth change in the trench below — an early, unverified
    warning with no known mechanism
  - If the updraft fails entirely (the rim goes cold), the whole flock leaves within a day and does not return
    until it resumes
  - Disturbed roosts scatter and do not re-form for a full turn of the moon, so a crew that scares them off
    loses the sign when it may matter most
Beaten by: not a threat; "beaten" means reading them right — Wits DC 12 to judge flock height against an
  ordinary morning, DC 16 to say by how much the warmth is changing and how soon
Shrugs off: capture or study up close — netted swifts stop flying the pattern within a day and tell a crew nothing
```

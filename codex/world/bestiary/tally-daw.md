# Tally-daw

A grey-white bird, indistinguishable from its neighbour down to the feather, that nests in grange rafters across
the [Ashfields](../geography/regions/the-ashfields.md). A grange's daws are always the same number as its granaries,
give or take a season's hatch, and nobody has found the bird that decides which.

## Stats

Might +0  Grace +3  Wits +1  Heart -1

## Wants

To keep the grange's grain accounted for. A tally-daw perches on a sack, a cart, or a stranger's shoulder and does
not move until whatever it is watching is either weighed in or gone. It wants nothing from a person who is not
touching grain.

## Presses

- **The clang.** Cross a threshold with unweighed grain and every daw in the rafters strikes its beak against iron
  hung there for exactly that purpose — a flat, ringing alarm that carries across a grange in seconds and brings
  every hand within earshot.
- **The mob.** Ignore the clang and the flock descends as one identical mass, driving for eyes and hands, not to
  wound but to make carrying anything impossible.
- **The follow.** A daw that has marked someone as a grain-thief will not stay behind. It rides a cart, a shoulder,
  a boat, for days, waiting for the grange to reclaim what it is owed.

## Beaten by

- **Weigh in first.** A tally-daw that watches grain crossed a scale and marked in a grange's ledger loses all
  interest — Wits contest DC 12 to convince one that this has already happened when it has not.
- **A grange's own word.** Any authority the grange itself recognises (a steward's seal, a grange-law writ) grounds
  the flock instantly. No roll — this is a fact the daws are built to know, not a thing to be persuaded of.
- **Waiting them out.** A marked daw gives up after leaving Ashfields soil, always exactly at the border. Nobody
  has explained why the border is where it stops mattering.

## Shrugs off

- **Killing one.** A dead tally-daw is replaced from the rafters within the hour, same size, same markings.
  Granges that have tried counting to see if the flock ever changes size report failure — the count is always
  exactly what it was.
- **Bribing with grain.** Feeding a tally-daw does not distract it. It is not hungry. It is counting.
